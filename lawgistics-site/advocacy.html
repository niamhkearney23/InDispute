Litigation Academy · Oral advocacy

Talk us through it

Pick a problem, read it, then record yourself arguing it out loud, the way you would to a partner or a judge. You get structured feedback on how you reason and how you present. Practise as often as you like.

What good sounds like

  1. Facts, what happened, and what the court below found.
  2. Issues, the real questions it turns on, not every complaint.
  3. The law, the principles that govern, including the standard on appeal.
  4. Who should win, and why, commit, and deal with the other side's best point.
  5. Your own view, what you found difficult or unpersuasive.
