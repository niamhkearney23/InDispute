Draft the letter

A letter of demand a partner would sign rather than rewrite. Read the file note, write the letter, and get it marked the way a supervising solicitor would mark it.

This exercise is set in Australia. The Malaysian one is not written yet, because a letter of demand is not the same letter in both places.

The file note

From
Your supervising solicitor
Client
Harbour Joinery Pty Ltd, a cabinetmaking business
Other side
Verrall Developments Pty Ltd, a builder
What happened
Our client fitted out fourteen apartments under a written subcontract dated 3 March. The work was completed on 19 June and the builder's site manager signed the completion sheet without noting any defect. Two invoices totalling $86,400 including GST were issued on 21 June, payable within 30 days. Nothing has been paid. On 2 August the builder's contracts manager said on the phone that "the developer hasn't paid us yet" and that they would "sort it out at the end of the job". Our client has since heard that the developer paid the builder in full in July. Our client wants the money and would prefer not to sue, but will if it comes to that.
Your task
Draft the letter of demand. Write the body of the letter only: no letterhead, no file references, no address block.
