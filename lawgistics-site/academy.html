On this site

Advocacy

Read a problem, argue it out loud, and get structured feedback on your reasoning, your structure and how you present. No account, nothing to install, and it is free while Lawgistics is in beta. The exercises are an appeal after trial, an appeal from an interlocutory decision, reading a judgment, and a problem you bring yourself, so they work the same in both countries.

In the app

Questions

A short diagnostic works out what you already know, then you get asked about what you do not. Answers are checked on the server rather than in your browser, and anything you get wrong comes back later until it stops being wrong. That needs an account, because the point of it is the record.

For firms

This page is the demonstration.

Everything above is the product running, not a mockup of it. Lawgistics uses its own academy to train its own people, which is the only recommendation worth much. Under your firm's name it would look like this, with your crest and your colour on it.

Your name on it

The name, the wordmark, the tagline and the accent colour are settings, not a fork. One firm, one deployment, your branding throughout. Your people never see ours.

Your induction, in your words

Your welcome, your AI policy and your joining checklist sit beside the training and never mix with it. An acknowledgement is pinned to the version somebody read, so republishing a policy puts it back in front of everyone.

A record that holds up

Nothing here concludes that a person is ready. A named supervisor decides, and the record keeps their name, the date, and how many items were still outstanding when they decided. Mistakes are corrected by recording a correction, never by deleting.

What we will not claim. This is one firm per deployment, not a platform serving many firms from one place. Every question carries its jurisdiction, and content is signed off by a named lawyer before it is published, which is slower than the alternative and the entire point.
