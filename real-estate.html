The scope

Sales agents and buyer's agents are in.

Acting in the sale or purchase of real estate for a client is a designated service. That covers selling agents, buyer's agents, and agents acting on developer listings.

The gap

REIV training isn't a program.

Industry CPD covers awareness — the Act requires a written program, an appointed AMLCO, SMR reporting, and an independent review. Different beast.

FAQ

Common questions from principals.

We're a small independent agency. Does this really apply to us? +

Yes — there's no turnover or headcount threshold for real estate. If you act for a vendor in selling property, or for a buyer in purchasing property, you're providing a designated service. A two-agent shop has the same statutory obligation as Ray White; only the proportionality of controls differs.

What about buyer's agents specifically? +

Buyer's agents are explicitly in scope. Acting in a real estate purchase for a client is a designated service in the same way as acting in a sale. Source-of-funds and beneficial ownership questions matter more for buyer's agents than for almost any other captured profession.

Does property management need a separate program? +

Pure rental management isn't a designated service on its own. The trigger is the sale/purchase work. If you do both under one entity, one program covers both — the controls just apply to the sales side. We'll scope this in the readiness check.

How much principal time does it actually take? +

For Done-With-You: a 60-minute scoping call, a 90-minute risk workshop, and roughly two hours reviewing the draft. We can usually go live within a principal-day of effort spread over two weeks.

Can you act as our external AMLCO? +

Yes — that's Tier 3. The AMLCO sits with us, reports to your principals, and handles AUSTRAC interactions. You keep selling property.
