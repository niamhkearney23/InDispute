The baseline

Bullion: in scope since 2006. DPMS: 1 July 2026.

Bullion dealers are an established reporting category. DPMS — dealers in precious metals and stones — come into scope from 1 July 2026 under Tranche 2. Pawnbrokers may also be captured where they provide designated services.

The risk

Cash, anonymity and portability.

FATF assesses bullion and high-value precious goods as a high ML/TF risk category. Cash settlements, easy resale, cross-border portability and historically light KYC make the sector attractive to bad actors.

The gap

Most shops have no compliance function.

Single shopfronts and small networks rarely have a dedicated compliance person. The owner is the AMLCO by default. We supply the framework, the named officer, and the periodic review.

The program

A program scaled to a single shopfront.

Proportionate controls. The Act doesn't require enterprise-grade systems in a two-staff bullion shop — but it does require the documented, repeatable process AUSTRAC can audit.

FAQ

Common questions.

Are pawnbrokers actually captured? +

It depends on what services you provide. Pure pawn-broking (a secured loan) isn't necessarily a designated service. But dealing in second-hand precious metals or stones over the threshold, or providing money transmission as an adjacent service, may be. We'll scope this in the first call.

We mostly do small retail. Do we need all this? +

Proportionate. A small-retail bullion operator needs counter procedures, TTR readiness, training and a documented program — but not a corporate AMLCO with a board reporting cadence. Our retainer is sized for that reality.

We're DPMS-only (no bullion). Different program? +

Mostly the same shape with sector-specific risk controls. See our dedicated DPMS page for the precious metals and stones-only build.
