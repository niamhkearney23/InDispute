# Competitive landscape memo — InfoTrack AML/CTF Compliance Centre

**Date:** May 2026  
**Status:** Strategy reset triggered

## The trigger

InfoTrack launched the **AML/CTF Compliance Centre** ahead of the 1 July 2026 deadline. Public messaging captured here:

- "Complimentary, all-inclusive solution that helps lawyers, conveyancers, accountants and real estate agents establish and operationalise their compliance framework."
- "Developed in collaboration with Grant Thornton Australia, advisers to AUSTRAC, and built from the official Starter Kits."
- "Fully integrated into your Practice Management System and is complimentary to use, with only search-based services charged."
- "Demonstrated to more than 8,000 practitioners nationwide and featured by 9News, 7NEWS and ABC News."
- VOI, PEP, sanctions, adverse media checks and bankruptcy/criminal history checks for the compliance officer are **free until 31 May 2026** — explicitly to capture the funnel.
- CPD-accredited training is included.
- Five obligation stages covered end-to-end: enrol, onboard firm + people, onboard customers, report to AUSTRAC, record-keeping.

## What this changes

| Original positioning | Reality post-InfoTrack |
|---|---|
| Productised AML/CTF programs for boutique law firms | InfoTrack delivers this for free, integrated into the DMS the firm already uses |
| $2,400 DIY template pack | Cannot compete with "free + already inside your software" |
| $9,500 Done-With-You build | Hard sell to a mainstream firm whose platform vendor provides equivalent at $0 |
| $2,800/mo Outsourced AMLCO | **Still defensible — a tool cannot form suspicion, sign off on EDD, or be a person named to AUSTRAC** |
| Tier 4 SaaS at $99–499/mo | Dead. InfoTrack has the same product, free, distributed via PMS integration, with Grant Thornton's name on it |
| Mainstream sector pages (law, accounting, conveyancing, real estate) | Lead with the human role, not the program build. These pages need rewriting. |

## What InfoTrack structurally cannot do

A platform vendor — by design — cannot:

1. **Be the named AMLCO** of a reporting entity (the person on the hook).
2. **Form suspicion** or make EDD judgement calls.
3. **Conduct independent reviews** of its own platform's outputs. The Act / Rules require independent third-party review at intervals; vendor-conducted reviews fail the independence test.
4. **Respond to AUSTRAC enforcement action** with privileged advice.
5. **Serve sectors outside its PMS partner footprint** — TCSPs, DPMS, gambling, MSBs, crypto, bullion, pawnbrokers. InfoTrack has no Securexchange-equivalent in those verticals.

These five gaps define the new strategy.

## New strategy: "the human, not the platform"

### Primary offers (everywhere, every sector)

1. **Outsourced AMLCO** ($2,800–4,500/mo) — named, qualified human appointed as the reporting entity's compliance officer. Sits on top of whatever platform the firm uses (InfoTrack, Themis, Lexis, in-house). The platform handles workflow; we make the judgement calls. Recurring revenue, defensible.
2. **Independent Reviews** ($4,500–12,000 per review) — third-party assurance against the program. Required at periodic intervals. **Cannot be done by the platform vendor.** Recurring. Defensible.
3. **Enforcement &amp; Crisis Response** (rate card, deposit retainer) — when AUSTRAC investigates, conducts an audit, or issues an infringement notice. Premium, lumpy, but high-margin and impossible to disintermediate.

### Niche-sector offers (sectors InfoTrack doesn't reach)

1. **TCSPs** (trust &amp; company service providers) — bespoke programs, AMLCO, training. No PMS platform serves them.
2. **DPMS** (dealers in precious metals &amp; stones) — same.
3. **Gambling &amp; wagering** — pre-existing Tier 1 obligations, ongoing regulatory pressure.
4. **MSBs &amp; remittance** — Tier 1 obligations, ongoing.
5. **Digital currency exchanges / crypto** — Tier 1 obligations, complex EDD, no boutique platform.
6. **Pawnbrokers, bullion dealers, second-hand high-value goods** — niche, often Tier 1 already.

In these sectors the full stack still applies — productised programs, training, AMLCO retainer — because no competitor is giving it away free.

### Channel relationship with InfoTrack

Worth a conversation. They have 8,000+ demonstrated firms; some will need an outsourced AMLCO. We can be the named delivery partner they refer to. Requires (a) the delivery partner (still our gating dependency) and (b) a credible introduction. Not the strategy — a sub-strategy.

## What we are dropping

- The Tier 4 self-serve SaaS direction. Mockup retired; product strategy doc updated.
- "Boutique law firms" as the primary positioning. Now positioned as one of several sectors, and only for firms where the InfoTrack baseline isn't sufficient.
- DIY templates as a standalone home-page tier. Moves to sector pages only (and only for sectors without a platform alternative).

## What we are doubling down on

- The partner search. **More urgent, not less.** A "the human, not the platform" positioning is meaningless without the human.
- The independent-reviews offer. Recurring revenue, structurally protected.
- Niche-sector content. TCSP, DPMS, gambling, MSB, crypto, bullion pages.

## Honest assessment

This is a smaller business than the original conception. The realistic ceiling for an outsourced-AMLCO + independent-reviews + niche-sector practice is something like 80–200 retainer clients and 100–250 reviews/year over five years — call it $4–10m ARR at maturity, not the $20m+ ARR a SaaS path implied.

But it's defensible against InfoTrack and Grant Thornton, the unit economics work, and it's something a single delivery partner + founder can credibly build. The original conception was — in retrospect — already too ambitious for the team and the moat.

The honest version is a better business to actually build.
