The rule

Reviews must be independent of program operation.

The Rules require the reviewer to have had no role in the design, implementation or ongoing operation of the program being reviewed.

Who's excluded

Anyone who built or runs your program.

Your AMLCO. Your platform vendor (including InfoTrack, Themis and similar). Your in-house compliance team. The consultant who drafted the program. All non-independent.

Who qualifies

A third party with the relevant expertise.

Internal staff in a different function may qualify for the smallest entities, but most reporting entities will need a qualified external reviewer who can demonstrate AML/CTF competence.

Each engagement assesses your program against the Rules, your own risk assessment, and a representative sample of your real-world records.

FAQ

Common questions.

How often do we need an independent review? +

The Rules require the review to occur at appropriate intervals proportionate to risk. For most low-to-medium-risk reporting entities, that's annual or bi-annual. Higher-risk businesses (TCSPs, MSBs, digital currency exchanges) should plan for annual. We'll recommend a cadence as part of the engagement.

Can our auditor or in-house compliance staff do the review? +

Sometimes. Internal staff in a function genuinely independent of the program (e.g. internal audit) may qualify for smaller entities. Most boutique firms don't have that internal segregation, and AUSTRAC has signalled it expects external review at maturity. Your statutory auditor isn't an AML/CTF expert — different competency.

Can the platform vendor (e.g. InfoTrack) do our review? +

No. A vendor reviewing the outputs of its own platform fails the independence test. The reviewer must be outside the supply chain that designed or operates the program.

What if you find serious problems? +

We rate findings by severity and explain the residual risk plainly. If findings rise to the level of mandatory disclosure (e.g. you've been failing to lodge required SMRs), we'll tell you, but the obligation to disclose sits with your AMLCO and board, not us. We're not the regulator. Our job is to give you a clear-eyed view; what you do with it is your decision.

Do you also remediate, or only identify? +

Independent reviews are intentionally separated from remediation, because the reviewer can't review work they did. If you want us to remediate, we'll introduce you to another qualified consultant or take you on as an outsourced AMLCO client — but the next review will be done by a third party.
