# 30-minute partner call — script

**Goal:** end the call knowing whether (a) you both want to talk again, (b) which commercial structure is the likely fit, and (c) what the partner needs from you to decide.

**Stance:** honest, not selling. The partner is qualifying you as much as you're qualifying them. Avoid overselling — the chassis is the proof.

---

## Before the call

Send 24 hours before:
- The site URL
- The directory URL
- The partner pitch document (PDF rendered from the HTML)

Have open in tabs:
- The site (so you can pull up specific pages)
- `commercial-structures.md`
- `partner-personas.md` (their persona — refresh before the call)
- Their LinkedIn profile

---

## 0:00 — Open (2 min)

"Thanks for the time. I want to spend the 30 minutes on three things: 5 minutes on you and what you're looking for next, 15 minutes on what's already built and what's missing, and 10 minutes on whether the commercial shape works for both of us. Sound right?"

If yes, continue. If they want to drive, let them drive.

## 2:00 — About them (5 min)

Open questions, not closed:

- What does your current role look like day-to-day?
- What would make you want to leave it?
- Have you thought about going independent, or partnering with someone, or moving to industry?
- What does your ideal next 18 months look like?
- What scares you about going independent?

Listen for:
- Real reasons to leave (vs casual interest)
- Tolerance for ambiguity / startup-stage shape
- Existing commercial obligations (clients, non-competes, notice periods)
- What they value (autonomy vs income vs reputation vs equity)

## 7:00 — What's built (12 min)

Walk them through the site live:
- Law-firm landing page — copy, credentials, pricing, founder slot
- Sector strip — show the four other ICPs as preview / coming soon
- Directory — show every artefact
- Big 4 channel pitch HTML — make the point that the GTM is serious
- Templates — open the program template, show the placeholder count

Be honest:
- "This is a sales surface. None of it has been delivered yet."
- "I built it because I see the market, not because I'm the AML expert."
- "The templates are credible drafts — they need someone with regulatory depth to pressure-test them."
- "The Big 4 pitch hasn't been sent yet."
- "Plausible analytics and Formspree are wired but I haven't bought the real form ID yet."

## 19:00 — Commercial shape (8 min)

"Three structures I'd entertain. Let me describe each in 30 seconds, then we can pull on whichever interests you."

Walk through Options A, B, C from `commercial-structures.md`. Keep it tight.

After the description:
- "Which of those looks closest to what you'd want?"
- "What would I have to change to make it work?"

If the partner picks A or B, ask:
- "What's the minimum equity / fee that would make this real for you?"
- "What's the maximum commitment you can make in the next 90 days?"

If they push back on the founder side equity in Option A:
- Don't negotiate live. "Fair — let me think on it and come back with a position by end of week. The chassis took real work and I want to be fair on both sides."

## 27:00 — Close (3 min)

"Quick wrap:

1. What's your gut on whether this is worth a second conversation?
2. If yes, what do you need from me to get to a yes?
3. Is there anyone in your network for whom this might be a better fit than you, regardless of your own answer?"

If yes to (1) — book the second call before you hang up. Don't leave it to email.

If maybe — agree a decision date. "Worth being honest about whether this works for you. Can we agree a yes/no by [date]?"

If no — thank them, ask for the referral in (3), close cleanly. The partner you didn't sign is sometimes the best introducer.

## Qualification — am I talking to the right person?

Track silently during the call:

| Signal | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| CAMS or equivalent | Stated, dated | "I've been meaning to" |
| Real Tier 2-equivalent delivery experience | Specific war stories | Abstract |
| Tranche 2 awareness | Has views, knows the EM | Generic |
| Comfortable being personally named on a program | Yes, expects it | Wants to stay anonymous |
| Existing book they can't fold in | Manageable | "It depends" |
| Decision-making timeline | Within 8 weeks | "Over the next year" |
| Equity literacy | Familiar with vesting / cliffs | Glazed over |

Three weak signals = polite pass. Bias toward signing fewer, better people than chasing a half-fit.

---

## Red flags to walk away from

- They want to be the founder/CEO. Wrong shape; politely thank them.
- They want to bring their existing practice in as part of ATTICUS without restructuring. That's a merger conversation, not a partnership.
- They want immediate cash compensation that's not aligned to runway. Either they don't believe in the upside or they don't believe in the founder.
- They talk past you, especially on the regulatory detail. They'll do that to clients too.
- They balk at being personally named as the AML lead. That's the whole job.
- They want to deliver but have someone junior do the writing. Not the practice we're building.

**Q: "Why are you doing this if you don't have AML background?"**
"Because the market is real, the deadline is real, and I'd rather have a real expert deliver it with me than represent capability I don't have. The risk of doing it the other way is bad for clients and bad for me."

**Q: "Why this particular structure / why now?"**
"Tranche 2 is the biggest expansion of the Act since 2006. The window for a productised entrant is 12-18 months — after that, the Big 4 and mid-tier downsells, or one of the incumbents scales, and the gap closes. The partner who comes in now has the option of being the named principal at the front of that wave."

**Q: "What happens if it doesn't work?"**
"Walk-away protections are written into the LOI. If 12 months in we don't have product-market fit, both sides can pause cleanly. Your equity vests proportionally. You keep your reputation intact — that's the whole point of being honest about what's built and what isn't."

**Q: "Have you signed anyone yet?"**
"Not yet. I want to sign the right delivery partner before I sign the first paid client. The reverse order is how this goes wrong."
