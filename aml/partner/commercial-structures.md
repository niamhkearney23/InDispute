# Commercial structures — co-founder, principal, employee

Three structures, openly described. Use this document during commercial discussions with a prospective partner. Pick one structure with the partner before signing an LOI.

---

## Option A — Co-founder (equity)

**Suits:** Persona 1 (Big 4 senior leaver) or Persona 2 (ex-regulator) committing primary professional time. Highest commitment, highest upside.

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Equity | 35–40% to partner · 60–65% to founder · negotiable based on stage and contribution |
| Vesting | 4 years, monthly, with 12-month cliff. Equity returned pro-rata if the partner leaves before fully vested. |
| Commitment | Full-time or 80% time, depending on the partner's existing obligations during transition. Partner exits parallel commitments within 90 days of signing. |
| Salary | Drawn equally between founders, sized to operating runway. Pre-revenue draw indicative $0–$8k/mo each. Increases as revenue lands. |
| Capital contribution | Founder underwrites first 6 months of operating costs (PI, AUSTRAC enrolment, software, contingency). No cash-in from partner. |
| Decision rights | Joint sign-off on hiring, equity issuance, any quote above $20k, brand or pricing changes, any client referred by the channel. |
| Bad-leaver protection | Standard. Equity treatment differs if partner leaves voluntarily, is removed for cause, or is removed without cause. |
| Reverse-vesting on founder | Yes — founder's equity also subject to a 12-month cliff. Symmetric. |
| IP | All AML-related IP held by the company. Pre-existing IP (the chassis already built, the partner's prior templates) licensed to the company. |
| Run-rate target | Profitable by month 12. If not, both sides have a contractual right to pause. |

**Why this works for the partner**
- Real equity, not phantom.
- They run the part of the business that determines its reputation and value.
- Walk-away protection if it doesn't work.

**Why this works for the founder**
- A genuine co-builder rather than a contractor.
- Aligned incentives on long-term defensibility.
- Cheaper in cash terms than option C while preserving more value than option B.

## Option B — Consulting principal (fee per engagement)

**Suits:** A partner who keeps an existing practice or other commitments and wants to test the fit without exiting their current role. Lower commitment, lower upside, faster start.

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Equity | None initially. Optional 15–25% equity grant after 12 months at the partner's election if engagement count exceeds 12 delivered Tier 2 / Tier 3 builds. |
| Fee model | 45% of net Tier 2 fee · 45% of net Tier 3 monthly retainer · 30% of net Tier 1 template fee. Paid within 14 days of client payment to ATTICUS. |
| Commitment | Per-engagement only. Partner can decline a referral. Partner cannot deliver competing engagements for the partner's own account inside ATTICUS's ICP for the term. |
| Capital contribution | Each side bears own costs. PI: partner carries primary PI on delivered work; ATTICUS carries practice-level PI as a backstop. |
| Decision rights | Partner has technical sign-off on every delivered artefact. Founder has commercial sign-off on every quote. |
| Brand | Partner is named on the site under "Principal Adviser" or similar. Not co-founder. |
| Term | 12 months, auto-renew. 90 days' notice to terminate, with in-flight engagements completed. |
| IP | Pre-existing partner IP remains the partner's. Anything developed inside an ATTICUS engagement is ATTICUS's. |
| AMLCO appointment (Tier 3) | Partner is named external AMLCO; sits with the partner not ATTICUS, with ATTICUS handling client management. |

**Why this works for the partner**
- Zero downside. Per-engagement economics.
- Keeps their existing book.
- Faster than negotiating an equity arrangement.

**Why this works for the founder**
- Get to delivered case studies faster.
- Test the working relationship before committing equity.
- Less negotiation friction.

**Why this is structurally weaker**
- Partner has no incentive to invest in product or process improvement beyond their own engagements.
- High risk of partner deciding to fork the practice once they see how the GTM works.
- Less defensible at the Big 4 channel pitch ("Who is your AML lead?" — "A consultant we use").

## Option C — Salaried principal

**Suits:** A partner who wants the steadiness of employment rather than equity / per-engagement. Less common but possible.

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Salary | $180,000–$230,000 AUD base depending on seniority and Tranche 2 specialism. Quarterly bonus of 5–10% of net revenue on engagements they lead. |
| Equity | Optional small grant (5–10%) at 12 months, vesting over 3 years. |
| Commitment | Full-time employee. Standard employment terms. |
| Capital contribution | None from partner. |
| Decision rights | Reports to founder commercially. Owns delivery decisions. |
| Brand | Partner is named on the site as "Principal" or "Director of Compliance". |
| Term | Employment contract — standard notice periods. |

**Why this works for the partner**
- Predictable income.
- Insulated from founder risk.
- Suits someone who's been a senior employee and wants the next role to be the same shape.

**Why this works for the founder**
- Total clarity on cost.
- Easier to swap if it doesn't work.

**Why this is structurally weaker**
- Hard to afford pre-revenue.
- Partner is not aligned to long-term value.
- Partner is incentivised to leave once they realise the GTM is the easier part and they could do this independently.

---

## Default opening position

When opening the commercial conversation:

1. Open with **Option A** at 35% equity.
2. If the partner is unwilling to commit primary time, move to **Option B** at 45%.
3. **Option C** only if they explicitly ask.

Avoid getting trapped in the gap between A and B. Pick one and structure cleanly.

---

## What's non-negotiable

Regardless of structure:

- Partner has technical sign-off on every artefact going to a client. No exceptions.
- Partner is named publicly within 30 days of LOI.
- ATTICUS does not represent capability we don't have.
- Founder underwrites pre-revenue operating costs (in A and C).
- Confidentiality and non-compete clauses written symmetrically.

---

## What changes once we have 5 paid clients

The economics of all three options improve materially. Re-open the structure at that milestone:

- Option A vesting accelerates by 6 months on the founder side as a goodwill gesture.
- Option B partner gets first refusal on equity conversion.
- Option C salaried partner gets the option of converting partial salary to equity.

This is written in the LOI from day one so it's not a renegotiation later.
