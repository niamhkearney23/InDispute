## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This document is the Firm's AML/CTF program prepared and maintained under Part 7 of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (Cth) ("the Act") and the AML/CTF Rules, including the unified-program rules effective from 1 July 2026. It sets out the systems, controls, policies and procedures the Firm uses to:

- identify, mitigate and manage the ML/TF risks the Firm reasonably faces in providing designated services;
- meet the Firm's obligations under the Act and Rules; and
- evidence to the partnership, AUSTRAC, the regulators of the legal profession, and the Firm's clients that the Firm operates a defensible compliance posture.

### 1.2 Designated services provided by the Firm
The Firm provides the following designated services within the meaning of section 6 of the Act:

- {{LIST_DESIGNATED_SERVICES}}

Examples typically engaged by a boutique law firm:
- buying or selling real estate for a client;
- managing client money, accounts, securities or other assets;
- acting as a formation agent for companies, partnerships, trusts or other legal arrangements;
- acting as, or arranging for another person to act as, a director, secretary, partner or trustee; and
- providing a registered office or business address for a client.

The Firm does **not** provide designated services other than those listed.

### 1.3 Scope and application
This program applies to all partners, employees, contractors, secondees, and volunteers of the Firm (together "personnel"). It applies to all matters and clients receiving a designated service.

## 3. ML/TF risk assessment

### 3.1 Approach
The Firm has assessed the ML/TF risk it reasonably faces in providing each of its designated services. The assessment considers, at a minimum:

- **Customers** — type, beneficial ownership transparency, PEP exposure, sanctions exposure, source-of-funds visibility;
- **Services** — designated services provided, including any cash-intensive or asset-transfer features;
- **Delivery channels** — face-to-face, remote / digital onboarding, intermediated;
- **Geography** — jurisdictions of customers, counterparties and source of funds, with regard to FATF and DFAT designations.

The detailed risk assessment is at **Annexure A**.

### 3.2 Risk rating
The Firm rates each client at onboarding as **Low**, **Medium** or **High**. Ratings are recorded in the matter management system and re-assessed at trigger events (see §5.5).

### 3.3 Frequency
The risk assessment is reviewed at least every 24 months and whenever:
- the Firm introduces a new designated service or material new client segment;
- a regulatory change materially affects the Firm's risk profile; or
- an incident or near-miss indicates the assessment may understate risk.

## 5. Customer due diligence (CDD)

### 5.1 When CDD applies
CDD is performed before the Firm provides a designated service to a client, except where a specific exemption in the Rules applies and is documented.

### 5.2 Initial CDD — minimum
For every client:
- collect full name, date of birth, residential address (individuals) or registered name, ABN/ACN, principal place of business and beneficial owners (entities);
- verify identity using a reliable independent source (e.g. {{ID_VERIFICATION_PROVIDER}} or government-issued documents);
- identify and verify beneficial owners holding 25% or more (or the lower threshold the Firm has adopted: {{BENEFICIAL_OWNER_THRESHOLD}});
- screen the client and beneficial owners against PEP and sanctions lists ({{SCREENING_PROVIDER}});
- understand and record the purpose and intended nature of the relationship and matter;
- record the source of funds and, where relevant, source of wealth.

### 5.3 Enhanced due diligence (EDD)
EDD applies to any client rated High and to any matter triggering one or more of the following:
- politically exposed persons (foreign, domestic or international organisation);
- nominee or complex ownership structures with limited transparency;
- jurisdictions on the FATF "high-risk" or "monitored" lists, or DFAT-sanctioned jurisdictions;
- transactions inconsistent with the client's known profile;
- cash settlement components above {{CASH_THRESHOLD}};
- requests for unusual structuring, urgency or secrecy;
- {{FIRM_SPECIFIC_TRIGGER_1}};
- {{FIRM_SPECIFIC_TRIGGER_2}}.

EDD additional steps include: senior management approval to onboard or continue the relationship; additional source-of-funds and source-of-wealth verification; closer ongoing monitoring; and shorter re-rating intervals.

### 5.4 CDD in matter intake
The matter intake workflow is set out at Annexure D and covers:
1. Conflict and AML risk pre-screen at first contact.
2. Client identification and verification.
3. Beneficial owner identification.
4. Risk rating and AMLCO sign-off where required.
5. Engagement letter issuance.
6. File opening with CDD evidence attached in {{DMS_NAME}}.

### 5.5 Ongoing customer due diligence
The Firm reviews and refreshes CDD information when:
- a new matter is opened more than 24 months after the previous matter;
- a material change in the client's circumstances comes to the Firm's attention;
- a transaction is inconsistent with the client's known profile;
- the matter triggers an EDD condition;
- on a periodic basis: every {{PERIODIC_REVIEW_LOW}} months for Low, {{PERIODIC_REVIEW_MEDIUM}} months for Medium, {{PERIODIC_REVIEW_HIGH}} months for High.

## 6. Reporting obligations

### 6.1 Suspicious matter reports (SMR)
Personnel who form a suspicion (or who hold information that would reasonably give rise to a suspicion) must immediately escalate to the AMLCO. The AMLCO determines whether to lodge an SMR with AUSTRAC within the statutory timeframe (24 hours for terrorism financing; 3 business days otherwise).

The Firm's red-flag list is at Annexure G. Personnel are reminded that tipping-off is a criminal offence; communications with the client about an escalation are prohibited unless cleared by the AMLCO in writing.

### 6.2 Threshold transaction reports (TTR)
The Firm lodges TTRs with AUSTRAC for any cash transaction of A$10,000 or more in connection with a designated service.

### 6.3 International funds transfer instructions (IFTI)
The Firm lodges IFTI reports where it sends or receives instructions for the transfer of funds into or out of Australia in the course of providing a designated service.

### 6.4 Channel
Reports are lodged through AUSTRAC Online by the AMLCO or their delegate. Login credentials are held by the AMLCO and one backup partner ({{BACKUP_PARTNER}}).

## 7. Recordkeeping

The Firm retains the following records for a minimum of seven years from the date the relevant matter ends or the record is created (whichever is later):

- CDD information and verification evidence;
- AML/CTF risk ratings and re-rating decisions;
- SMR, TTR and IFTI reports and supporting decision logs;
- training records and attestations;
- this program and all prior versions;
- AMLCO reports to the {{BOARD_OR_PARTNERSHIP}};
- independent review reports.

Records are stored in {{DMS_NAME}} under the AML/CTF tag set, with a retention rule applied. Annexure H maps each record type to its storage location.

## 9. Independent review

The Firm commissions an independent review of this program:
- within 12 months of first adoption; and
- at least every 3 years thereafter, or sooner if directed by the AMLCO or {{BOARD_OR_PARTNERSHIP}}.

The reviewer must be independent of the design and operation of the program. The review report is provided to the {{BOARD_OR_PARTNERSHIP}} and any material findings tracked to closure.
