# Red-flag indicators — by practice area

**Annexure G to the AML/CTF Program · {{FIRM_LEGAL_NAME}}**
Version {{VERSION}} · Approved {{APPROVAL_DATE}}

> Personnel are trained to recognise the indicators below as **possible** signs of ML/TF. A single indicator is not in itself a basis for an SMR — but it is a basis for escalation to the AMLCO, who then determines whether suspicion has been formed.

---

## Cross-practice indicators (all matters)

### Client behaviour
- Reluctance to provide identification or beneficial ownership details when requested.
- Use of nominees, intermediaries, complex ownership structures or jurisdictions with limited transparency, with no apparent business reason.
- Source of funds is inconsistent with the client's known occupation, business or wealth profile.
- Unusual interest in our reporting obligations, AML/CTF procedures or what triggers an SMR.
- Pressure to complete a matter urgently with no commercial rationale.
- Willingness to pay above-market fees, particularly in cash or via opaque routes, for no apparent advantage.
- Recent jurisdictional connection (residence, business, source of funds) with a FATF "high-risk" or "monitored" jurisdiction, or a DFAT-sanctioned country.

### Transactional indicators
- Funds received from or paid to a third party with no clear link to the matter.
- Use of multiple bank accounts (especially across jurisdictions) for what should be a single transaction.
- Round-sum cash payments where a banked equivalent would be expected.
- Cancellation of a transaction at the late stage where the client is willing to accept a financial loss.
- Settlement in cryptocurrency or precious metals where conventional payment would normally be used.

## By practice area

### Property &amp; conveyancing
- Buyer is anonymous, represented entirely by an intermediary, or refuses to be introduced.
- Buyer makes the offer above asking price with limited inspection.
- Use of a newly-incorporated entity or a shell trust with no operating history.
- Source of deposit is from a jurisdiction unrelated to the buyer's known affairs.
- Buyer plans to settle the balance partly or wholly in cash.
- Quick on-sale ("flip") immediately after settlement, particularly at a loss.
- Vendor is willing to accept significantly below market value with no commercial rationale.
- Foreign buyer not subject to FIRB approval where one would normally be expected (potentially indicating nominee structure).

### Trusts &amp; estates / private client
- Establishment of a discretionary trust where the named appointor and beneficiaries are not the apparent ultimate beneficial owner.
- Loans into family trusts from offshore entities the client is reluctant to identify.
- Cross-border estate elements with assets in opaque jurisdictions.
- Cash gifts in or out of estate matters above plausible thresholds.
- Pressure to vary trust deeds in ways that change effective control without changing apparent beneficial ownership.

### Corporate / commercial
- Company formation where the directors and beneficial owners differ in non-obvious ways.
- Use of bearer shares (where still permitted) or nominee shareholding structures.
- Restructuring requests with no apparent tax, commercial or estate rationale.
- Asset transfers between related entities at non-arm's-length values.
- Acquisition of dormant companies (shelf companies acquired for their history rather than re-incorporated).

### Family law (with property elements)
- Settlement terms that move materially more value to one party than the apparent financial position justifies.
- Late discovery of offshore or trust-held assets the disclosing party cannot easily explain.
- Settlement payments via third parties or via cryptocurrency.
- Use of consent orders to transfer property to entities controlled by third parties.

## Escalation

Any personnel observing one or more indicators must escalate **immediately** to the AMLCO ({{AMLCO_NAME}}) using the internal escalation form (Annexure I). Personnel must **not** discuss the escalation or any contemplated SMR with the client; tipping-off is a criminal offence under the Act.
