Readiness checklist · 12 questions · ~8 minutes

Is your firm ready for 1 July 2026?

Twelve plain-English questions. You'll get a readiness score, the gaps in priority order, and a one-page brief you can take to your next leadership meeting.

1 — Scope

Q1. Have you confirmed which of your matter types are "designated services" under the AML/CTF Act?

e.g. real estate transactions, managing client money, forming companies/trusts, acting as nominee.

Q2. Have you enrolled (or applied to enrol) with AUSTRAC?

Enrolment was due 29 March 2026 for legal practitioners providing designated services.

Q3. Do you have a documented ML/TF risk assessment for the firm?

Covering customers, services, delivery channels, geography, and the risk of being misused for ML/TF.

2 — Governance

Q4. Has the firm appointed an AML/CTF Compliance Officer (AMLCO)?

A specific named person with the authority and seniority to make compliance decisions.

Q5. Does the partnership / board oversee the AML/CTF program with a regular reporting cadence?

Quarterly reporting is the typical baseline.

Q6. Is there a written, board-approved AML/CTF program covering the unified-program requirements?

Single program (no Part A / Part B split) under the new rules.

3 — Program controls

Q7. Do you have a documented customer due diligence (CDD) procedure built into your client intake?

ID verification, beneficial owners, source of funds, PEP and sanctions screening.

Q8. Do you have an enhanced due diligence (EDD) trigger framework for high-risk clients or matters?

Q9. Do you have suspicious matter reporting (SMR) procedures and access to AUSTRAC Online?

Q10. Have all partners and staff completed AML/CTF training in the last 12 months, with attestations on file?

4 — Operational readiness

Q11. Do you have a 7-year recordkeeping schedule mapped to your DMS, practice management or CRM system?

Q12. Have you scoped (or scheduled) the first independent review of the program?

Required periodically once the program is operational.
