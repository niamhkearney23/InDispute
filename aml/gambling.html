The baseline

Tier-one obligation since 2006.

Wagering services, totalisator agency operations, gaming machines in clubs and casinos, lotteries and online wagering are all designated services. Your enrolment isn't new — but the standard AUSTRAC expects has moved.

The enforcement reality

Crown, Star and SkyCity reset the bar.

The casino matters established that AUSTRAC will pursue large penalties for deficient programs, even where there's no proven money-laundering offence. Inadequate AMLCO authority and weak source-of-funds controls were central.

The gap

Mid-tier operators carry the same exposure.

Smaller wagering and gaming operators have similar obligations but a fraction of the in-house compliance team. The exposure is concentrated in a single AMLCO who may not have the bandwidth or AUSTRAC-grade experience to defend the program.

FAQ

Common questions from operators.

Our AMLCO is internal. Why outsource? +

For mid-sized operators, an internal AMLCO often holds the role alongside another full-time job and rarely has AUSTRAC-investigation-grade experience. Outsourcing — or supplementing the internal AMLCO — brings sector-experienced judgement at a fraction of a full-time hire.

Does the Crown / Star benchmark really apply to us? +

Yes — the program standards in those proceedings are now the de-facto bar AUSTRAC will look for, scaled to your size. The control expectations don't change; the implementation does.

We're online-only. Different rules? +

Same statutory obligations; different risk profile. Online wagering needs stronger digital-channel CDD, device-fingerprint linking, deposit-source controls and a tighter EDD trigger framework. We build for the channel mix you actually operate.
