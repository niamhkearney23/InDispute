# FEATURED: Hanson v Faruqi (Full Court, 27 July 2026)

## 1. Quick Summary

- **Court:** Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia
- **Bench:** three judges, unanimous (names to be confirmed from the judgment; FCAFC citation number pending indexing)
- **Practice Area:** Discrimination / Constitutional law / Media & speech
- **Decision Date:** 27 July 2026
- **Citation:** FCAFC number TBC; on appeal from *Faruqi v Hanson* [2024] FCA 1264 (Stewart J)

**30-word summary:** The Full Federal Court unanimously dismissed Pauline Hanson's appeal: her "piss off back to Pakistan" post to Senator Faruqi breached s 18C, and s 18C survived her constitutional challenge.

## 2. The Story

In September 2022, hours after the Queen's death, Greens Senator Mehreen Faruqi posted her views about the monarchy. Senator Pauline Hanson replied on X, telling her to "pack your bags and piss off back to Pakistan."

Senator Faruqi sued under section 18C of the Racial Discrimination Act, which makes it unlawful to publicly offend, insult, humiliate or intimidate a person because of race, colour or national or ethnic origin. In 2024, Justice Angus Stewart found the post was "seriously offensive," a strong form of racism, and unlawful.

Hanson appealed everything: the finding itself, and the law it rested on. She argued s 18C is constitutionally invalid because it burdens the implied freedom of political communication.

On 27 July, three judges of the Full Court answered, unanimously: the post was correctly characterised as anti-Muslim and Islamophobic, a variant of the racist slogan "go back to where you came from." Section 18C is valid. The fair comment defence failed. Appeal dismissed.

## 3. Why This Matters

Section 18C has been a political football for two decades, and its opponents' strongest legal argument has always been the constitutional one. That argument was just tested at full strength and rejected by an intermediate appellate court, unanimously. Only the High Court can disturb this now, and Hanson has 28 days to seek special leave; her camp has signalled she will.

For everyone else, the practical message is blunt: political context does not convert racial abuse into protected political communication. Public figures, employers and platforms should assume s 18C means what it says.

## 4. Key Legal Principles

- Section 18C RDA prohibits public acts reasonably likely to offend, insult, humiliate or intimidate because of race or ethnicity; the bar sits at serious and profound effects, which this post met.
- Characterisation is objective: the Full Court upheld findings that the post carried an anti-Muslim, Islamophobic message in the tradition of "go back to where you came from."
- Section 18C does not impermissibly burden the implied freedom of political communication; the challenge to validity failed.
- The s 18D fair comment exemption requires more than the speaker asserting public-interest commentary; it was not established here.
- Intermediate appellate authority now squarely supports validity; change requires the High Court.

## 5. Practical Takeaway

If you're acting for a client tomorrow: treat s 18C as constitutionally solid. Advising media, politicians or anyone with a platform: the "political speech" shield does not cover racially targeted attacks, and fair comment must be genuinely made out, not asserted. If your client faces an 18C complaint, focus on the objective characterisation of the words and any s 18D exemption evidence early. And watch the special leave window: if the High Court takes this, it becomes the speech case of the decade.

# Hanson v Faruqi: Full Federal Court Upholds Section 18C Ruling and Rejects Constitutional Challenge

## The decision

On 27 July 2026, the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia unanimously dismissed Senator Pauline Hanson's appeal against the finding that her September 2022 social media post telling Senator Mehreen Faruqi to "pack your bags and piss off back to Pakistan" contravened section 18C of the Racial Discrimination Act 1975 (Cth). The Court also rejected Hanson's argument that s 18C is constitutionally invalid.

## Background

The post was made in reply to Senator Faruqi's comments following the death of Queen Elizabeth II. At first instance in 2024, Justice Angus Stewart found the post was "seriously offensive," constituted a strong form of racism, and was reasonably likely to offend, insult, humiliate or intimidate Muslim migrants of colour in Australia (*Faruqi v Hanson* [2024] FCA 1264).

## The appeal

Hanson challenged both the finding and the law. Her constitutional argument contended s 18C impermissibly burdens the implied freedom of political communication. The Full Court disagreed on every front: the primary judge correctly characterised the post as carrying an anti-Muslim, Islamophobic message, a variant of the historically racist slogan "go back to where you came from"; s 18C is valid; and the fair comment exemption in s 18D was not established.

## What happens next

Hanson has 28 days to seek special leave to appeal to the High Court of Australia and has indicated she may. Unless and until special leave is granted, the Full Court's decision stands as strong intermediate appellate authority for the validity and operation of s 18C.

## What it means for you

- **Public figures and commentators:** political context does not immunise racially targeted speech.
- **Employers and platforms:** s 18C complaints should be assessed on the objective meaning of the words, not the speaker's asserted intent.
- **Practitioners:** the constitutional question is settled at this level; strategy in 18C matters should focus on characterisation and s 18D evidence.
