## 1. Farrugia v The King [2026] HCA 28

**High Court of Australia, 12 August 2026. On appeal from the NSW Court of Criminal Appeal (S139/2025).**

**Confirmed facts and issue.** Senior Counsel appeared at a sentencing hearing for **both the appellant and a co-offender**. Counsel submitted that the appellant's offending was more serious than the co-offender's, contrary to the Crown's characterisation, while also submitting that both offences were less serious than the Crown contended.

The question was whether a conflict of interest arose from one counsel appearing for two offenders in the same sentencing proceeding, where submissions on **parity** between them favoured one and were adverse to the other, and whether that produced practical injustice or a material irregularity.

The threshold is confirmed: for an irregularity in the sentencing process to be material, it must be one that **"could realistically" have affected the sentence imposed**.

The Court of Criminal Appeal below was constituted by Price AJA, Campbell and Hamill JJ.

**Not confirmed.** The outcome. One search summary reported that the appellant did not succeed and that any irregularity was not material. A later, differently worded search returned that the matter was undecided, which is now wrong given the judgment has issued, and that inconsistency is exactly why this is not going on a slide. **Nothing has been published by us either way.**

**Why it is worth waiting for.** This is a professional conduct case with a clean fact pattern, and it lands on a subject students actually sit: legal ethics and the duty to avoid conflicts. One barrister, two clients being sentenced together, and a parity argument that necessarily ranks one above the other. As soon as the outcome is confirmed this becomes a strong single case post with an obvious learning line.
