**LinkedIn post:**

A trade mark fight between B&amp;Q and Wren Kitchens produced a ruling this week that every commercial litigator will recognise, and most law students never see.

B&amp;Q Ltd v Wren Kitchens Ltd [2026] EWHC 2552 (BP), High Court, Business and Property Courts, David Stone sitting as a Deputy High Court Judge.

The background: B&amp;Q sues for trade mark infringement under sections 10(1) and 10(3) of the Trade Marks Act 1994 over eight sets of advertisements comparing kitchen prices. Wren applied under paragraph 18 of Practice Direction 57AD to vary the Disclosure Review Document, saying most of its requests flowed from new counterclaims concerning in-store price stands and staff showing customers a review website. B&amp;Q had offered further searches of two stores and of head office custodians for late 2017. Wren wanted wider date ranges, more custodians and more keywords.

The numbers did the work. B&amp;Q told the court that more than 600,000 documents had been ingested, 27,541 reviewed and 1,364 disclosed, at a cost above &pound;600,000, and that Wren was seeking to rerun searches because it disliked the outcome. Disclosure budgets in the case approach &pound;1.4 million.

The outcome: the application was largely refused on proportionality grounds. One custodian was added, a junior employee who had been asked to coordinate B&amp;Q&rsquo;s activity on the issue and who might hold documents not found elsewhere.

The principle worth carrying into practice: an order for extended disclosure will be varied only where the variation is necessary for the just disposal of the proceedings and is reasonable and proportionate. The Disclosure Review Document is not a draft. It is the architecture of the case, and a disappointing yield is not a ground to rebuild it.

The infringement claim and the counterclaims remain undecided, so nothing in this ruling tells us anything about the adverts themselves.

For those who litigate: how much of your disclosure strategy is settled before the DRD is signed, and how often does a later variation application actually earn its cost?
