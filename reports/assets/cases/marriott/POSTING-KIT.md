## ⚠️ Two rules for this one

**The assault is alleged, not proven.** This is an interlocutory ruling on whether claims can proceed. Slide 1 says "she says", slide 5 does nothing but make the point. Never let a caption or a reply turn it into a finding.

**Nobody is named, and the party is not named either.** The judgment says the Victorian Branch of the Australian Labor Party, and that is public. The captions below say "a political party" instead, on purpose. The legal point does not need it, and naming it turns the comments into a political argument instead of a discussion about duty of care. Your call, but I would keep it out.

## Instagram caption (copy-paste)

Can you sue the people who watched?

A woman says she was assaulted at a political party branch meeting in October 2023. She sued the man she says assaulted her. She also sued two fellow members, in negligence, for failing to protect her.

That second claim is the interesting one.

A political party is an unincorporated association: a group with no legal existence separate from its members. Nobody is the company. And there is no recognised duty of care between members of one, absent some other relationship. Being in the same club does not make you responsible for what happens in the room.

So she had to build a duty from scratch out of ordinary negligence principles. Foreseeability. Knowledge. Control. Assumption of responsibility.

Against one defendant it failed. He had become an office holder only weeks before the meeting, never attended the branch meetings, and there was no evidence he had been told anything or taken anything on. Her own counsel conceded there was no evidence of an assumption of responsibility. That claim was summarily dismissed.

Against the other member it survived. She says she raised concerns months earlier about another member behaving in a hostile way, and that this member said she would keep an eye on it and pass it on. If that is right, knowledge and an assumption of responsibility are at least arguable, and that is a question for a trial rather than a summary application.

Her pleading was still struck out, as vague and internally inconsistent. She has leave to redo it.

What turns a relationship into a duty is not proximity. It is knowing, and then taking something on.

Nothing has been decided about the assault. This ruling was only about whether the claims can go forward at all.
