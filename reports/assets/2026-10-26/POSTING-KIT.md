A quiet arbitration judgment from last week is worth five minutes of any commercial lawyer&rsquo;s time.

On 22 October 2026 the Federal Court of Australia published LandOcean Hong Kong Investment Holding Group Ltd v West Indies Exploration Company Ltd (No 2) [2026] FCA 1574, a decision of Justice Stewart.

The background, as recorded in the Court&rsquo;s own catchwords, is familiar to anyone who works in cross border disputes. Orders had been made ex parte to enforce a foreign arbitral award. The respondents then applied to set those orders aside, claiming that enforcement would result in &ldquo;double recovery&rdquo; and would therefore be inconsistent with public policy.

The interlocutory application was dismissed.

Two things stand out for students and early career lawyers.

First, enforcement of a foreign award is designed to be swift, and the ex parte route exists precisely so that an award creditor is not stalled at the starting line.

Second, public policy is not a general appeal right. A party resisting enforcement carries the onus and has to fit itself inside a recognised ground of refusal. Commercial discomfort with the outcome is not enough.

If you are drafting a dispute resolution clause this quarter, that is the shadow you are negotiating in.

Full reasons are published on the Federal Court&rsquo;s judgments site.
