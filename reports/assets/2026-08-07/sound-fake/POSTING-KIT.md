## Instagram caption (copy-paste)

Five more. All real. All citable.

1. **Grant v Australian Knitting Mills** [1936] AC 85. A doctor in Adelaide bought woollen underwear with excess sulphite left in the fabric, got dermatitis so severe he spent 17 weeks in bed, and sued. The Privy Council held the manufacturer liable and extended the snail-in-the-bottle principle beyond food. You will meet this one in first year torts.

2. **Twentieth Century Fox v South Australian Brewing Co** (1996). An Australian brewer started selling Duff Beer. The name belonged to a cartoon. Fox and Matt Groening sued and won on passing off.

3. **Cadbury Schweppes v Darrell Lea** [2008] FCA 470. Cadbury went to court to try to own the colour purple for chocolate. Justice Heerey said no. Cadbury appealed, won a retrial, and lost again.

4. **Kraft v Bega** [2020] FCAFC 65. Kraft sold its Australian peanut butter business to Bega, then tried to re-enter the market using the same yellow lid. The Full Federal Court held the trade dress travelled with the goodwill. The jar is Bega's.

5. **Deckers v Australian Leather**. An Australian company argued "ugg" is a generic word for a sheepskin boot, because in Australia it is. The US courts said generic there does not make it generic here. $450,000 in damages.

The weird ones are usually the ones that changed something.

General information only, not legal advice.

## Verification

Every case checked against secondary sources this session.

| Case | Confirmed |
|---|---|
| Grant v Australian Knitting Mills [1936] AC 85 | Privy Council, judgment 21 October 1935, purchase June 1931, excess sulphite, 17 weeks bedridden, manufacturer and retailer both liable |
| Twentieth Century Fox v South Australian Brewing Co & Lion Nathan (1996) | Federal Court, Tamberlin J, Duff Beer launched 1995, passing off and Trade Practices Act, Fox succeeded |
| Cadbury Schweppes v Darrell Lea (No 8) [2008] FCA 470 | Heerey J, 2006 first instance loss, 2007 Full Court appeal allowed and remitted, 2008 retrial, Cadbury lost again |
| Kraft Foods Group Brands LLC v Bega Cheese Ltd [2020] FCAFC 65 | Full Federal Court dismissed Kraft's appeal, Bega entitled to the trade dress, High Court refused special leave |
| Deckers Outdoor Corp v Australian Leather | US District Court (N.D. Ill.) 2018 summary judgment for Deckers, $450,000 damages, Federal Circuit affirmed 2021, Supreme Court denied certiorari |

**Two honesty notes for the caption if anyone challenges it:**
- The ugg case was decided in **United States** courts, not Australian ones. The slide says so. Do not let it drift into "an Australian court banned the word ugg", which is false.
- Grant is a **Privy Council** decision on appeal from the High Court of Australia, so it is Australian in origin but not an Australian court. The slide credits the Privy Council.
