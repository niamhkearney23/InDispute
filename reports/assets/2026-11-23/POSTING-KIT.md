On 20 November 2026 Justice Lee approved the settlement of the Williams class action against Toyota Motor Corporation Australia Limited: a total sum of $750 million, inclusive of legal costs and any payment to the litigation funder.

The interesting part is what the Court declined to do. The releases, covenants and other promises purportedly given on behalf of group members were not approved. Late registrants were let in. A referee will report on a competitive tender of the settlement administration.

Read that as a map of where judicial supervision actually bites. Group members are not at the negotiating table, so s 33V is where the Court sits in their chair. Approving a sum of money and authorising every clause the parties signed are two different judicial acts, and only one of them was on offer here.

**LinkedIn post:**

A $750 million class action settlement was approved in the Federal Court on Friday. The more instructive story is the part that was not.

In Williams v Toyota Motor Corporation Australia Limited (Settlement Approval) [2026] FCA 1781, Justice Lee approved the settlement of the representative proceeding under s 33V(1) of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth). The total settlement sum is $750 million, inclusive of legal costs and any payment to the litigation funder. Deductions for the applicants&rsquo; legal costs were allowed under s 33V(2).

Then the orders narrow. The releases, covenants and other promises purportedly given on behalf of group members were not approved. An order under s 33ZF authorising the applicants, retrospectively, to enter into and give effect to the deed was refused. The quantum of the funder&rsquo;s deduction was reserved. Late registrants were permitted to participate. A referee will report on a competitive tender of the settlement administration.

For anyone working in class actions, that last cluster is the practical takeaway: the Court is scrutinising the cost of administering a settlement, the price of funding it, and the scope of what a representative party can promise for people who were never in the room.

For students, it is a clean illustration of a point that gets flattened in textbooks. Settlement approval is a supervisory jurisdiction, not a signature. The Court can approve the deal and still decline to clothe the surrounding instrument with its authority.

The principle worth memorising: approval of a class action settlement under s 33V does not itself validate releases or promises purportedly given on behalf of group members.
