**LinkedIn post:**

Davis v Wilson [2026] FCAFC 142 was delivered by the Full Court of the Federal Court on 18 November 2026, before Markovic, Sarah C Derrington and Bennett JJ, and it rewards a close read by anyone who sits near a board table or an audit file.

The proceeding was a representative action alleging that financial reports overstated biological assets: sandalwood plantations, valued under AASB 12 and AASB 141. Everything turned on the assumed predicted heartwood yield per tree, with the applicants contending that a market participant would have adopted 6 to 8 kg per tree.

Three takeaways.

1. Pleadings. The Full Court held that the particulars formed part of the pleaded case, and that the primary judge erred in construing the pleadings. The applicants therefore had to establish the 6 to 8 kg counterfactual yield. They did not, and the valuation assumptions were not shown to be unreasonable.

2. Directors. An unqualified collective declaration under s 295 of the Corporations Act conveyed that each approving director held the stated opinions. Reliance on internal expertise, the audit committee and the external auditor was part of the question of reasonable grounds, not a reason to treat the declaration as impersonal.

3. Auditors. Audit opinions and audit services had a sufficient connection with the company&rsquo;s shares to be &ldquo;in relation to&rdquo; a financial product for s 1041H of the Corporations Act and ss 12BAB and 12DA of the ASIC Act. On the facts, contravention was not established: the audits complied with the Australian Auditing Standards and the unqualified opinions had a reasonable foundation.

The appeal was dismissed, the cross-appeals were allowed in part, and the appellant was ordered to pay the respondents&rsquo; costs.

If a collective declaration speaks for every director who approves it, how much of your board&rsquo;s financial reporting process is genuinely testing the numbers, and how much is trusting the person who prepared them?
