**LinkedIn post:**

Davis v Wilson [2026] FCAFC 142, handed down by the Full Court of the Federal Court today, is a useful reminder that misleading conduct cases are won on two separate questions.

The background: a representative proceeding alleging that a company&rsquo;s financial reports overstated the value of its biological assets, namely its sandalwood plantations. The valuation turned on the predicted heartwood yield per tree. The applicants contended that a market participant would have adopted a yield of 6 to 8 kilograms per tree.

The Full Court held that the counterfactual yield was not established and that the valuation assumptions were not shown to be unreasonable.

The auditor findings are the part worth reading twice. The Court held there was a sufficient connection between the audit opinions and the company&rsquo;s shares, which brought the conduct within the misleading conduct provisions of the Corporations Act and the ASIC Act. That is a meaningful conclusion about reach. It did not decide the case. The audits complied with the Australian Auditing Standards, the unqualified opinions had a reasonable foundation, and no contravention was established.

The appeal was dismissed, the cross-appeals were allowed in part, and the appellant was ordered to pay the respondents&rsquo; costs.

For students and juniors, the structural point is the transferable one: establishing that conduct falls within a prohibition is a threshold, not a verdict. The real work is proving the statement lacked a reasonable foundation when it was made.
