A regulator lost an appeal in the Full Federal Court yesterday, and the reasoning path is more useful than the result.

<b>Australian Securities and Investments Commission v Cohen</b> [2026] FCAFC 144 concerned incentives offered to sales agents in a call centre, and whether those incentives amounted to conflicted remuneration under Division 4 of Part 7.7A of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth).

The issues, in order:

1. Did the sales agents provide financial product advice?
2. Was the presumption of conflicted remuneration in section 963L engaged?
3. If engaged, was the presumption rebutted?
4. Was there conflicted remuneration within the meaning of section 963A(b), and contraventions of sections 963E(2), 963F and 963J?
5. Did a director breach section 180, and was that director knowingly involved in the contraventions?

On 16 November 2026, O&rsquo;Bryan, Anderson and Downes JJ dismissed the appeal with costs.

For anyone advising on incentive design, the practical point is that conflicted remuneration analysis is not confined to licensed advisers in advice meetings. It follows the conduct, the benefit and the structure of the scheme, which means remuneration design sits squarely in the compliance conversation rather than beside it.

For students, the transferable skill is sequencing. A presumption is a starting point, not a finding, and the party relying on it still has to carry the steps that come before and after.
