## What I took, and what I did not

**Taken, because it is the actual mechanism:**

- **Centred layout.** Their whole format is centred, ours has always been left aligned. This is the biggest visual change and it is what makes it read as news rather than as analysis.
- **One story per slide, nothing else.** Big serif headline, a visual object, three or four lines, the source. No learning line, no jargon arrow, no second paragraph.
- **A visual object in the middle.** In theirs it is a photo. That block in the centre of the slide is doing real work: it breaks the wall of text and gives the eye somewhere to land.
- **The keyword rail on the cover.** Theirs reads "investing · productivity · business". Ours reads "judgments · penalties · appeals". It tells a new viewer what the account is in one glance.
- **A positioning line on the closer.** Theirs is "Finance news, without the finance bros?". Ours is "Australian judgments, in plain English."

**Not taken, and here is why:**

**1. No photos.** This is the one real limitation. They use press shots of Zuckerberg and Altman. We cannot do the equivalent, for two reasons. There is no licence for press photography, and more importantly, a CEO's face on a finance post is a very different thing from a litigant's face on a legal post. Farrugia and Ko are real people, one of whom is awaiting a retrial. Their faces are not going on a slide.

So the centre block is a **verdict chip** instead: the outcome in letterspaced caps inside a thin rule, with a big number where there is one. It does the same layout job. If you ever want an actual image in there, the safe versions are your own photography, a public building, or a plain graphic.

**2. The follow prompt is a deviation from your own rules.** Slide 6 says "follow @lawgisticsaustralia". Your rules say a confident offer is stated once and left alone, and the close is the offer. I have put it in because it is central to how their format works and you asked for their format. **Delete that one line if it reads as needy to you.** The offer underneath it still stands on its own.

**3. The tone.** Theirs is "Girl, did you miss the finance tea this week?". I did not write that, because your house voice is restrained and premium and that is a genuine repositioning rather than a tweak.

If you do want it warmer, the cover line is the only thing that has to change. Options, in ascending order of chat:

- **"Did you miss what happened in court?"** ← built
- "You missed a big month in court."
- "Everyone missed this month in court."
- "Did you miss the court tea?"

Change that one line and the rest of the deck carries either voice.

**4. "This week" became "August 2026".** Deliberate. You renamed this format to Recently in Court precisely so it stops claiming to be today's news. Three of these are from 12 August and one from 28 July. The cover names the real window.

## Whether to switch the format permanently

Do not decide from one post. Run this one and the left-aligned version of the same content is already sitting in `reports/assets/cases/process/`, so you have a genuine comparison.

What this format is better at: reach, new viewers, and legibility at a glance. What the existing format is better at: authority, saves, and looking like nothing else on the feed. The centred news style looks more like everyone else, which is exactly why it travels further and exactly why it is less distinctive.

A reasonable answer is to keep both. News style for the roundup, house style for single case deep dives. They are different jobs.
