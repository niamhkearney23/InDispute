**LinkedIn post:**

When does "part of an industry" actually mean part of an industry? The High Court just answered that for coal mining long service leave, and the reasoning travels well beyond mining.

Coal LSL runs the portable long service leave scheme for Australia's black coal mining industry, funded by levy contributions from employers. Orica's shotfiring and explosive services employees work at black coal mines, but Orica argued they sat outside the scheme because of the broader nature of its business. The Full Federal Court agreed with Orica in 2025, limiting the workers' eligibility.

Coal LSL took it to the High Court. Five judges, Gageler CJ, Gordon, Steward, Gleeson and Jagot JJ, unanimously overturned that result. Shotfiring, they held, is an intrinsic and essential part of coal mining. Eligibility under the scheme turns on the nature and location of the work performed, not how broad the employer's overall business happens to be.

The practical effect: Orica's shotfirers are eligible employees, covered by the Black Coal Mining Industry Award, and Orica owes levy contributions on that basis.

For anyone advising on industry-specific statutory schemes, workers compensation, portable leave, industry funds, the test the Court applied here is the one to remember: look at what the person actually does and where, not what else the employer does.

What do you think, should coverage under schemes like this ever turn on the employer's overall business rather than the work itself?
