**LinkedIn post:**

The most consequential tech law hearing in the world right now is happening in Canberra, and it is not about privacy, antitrust or AI.

Australia&rsquo;s minimum age law took effect last December. Children under 16 cannot hold accounts on Reddit, Facebook, Instagram, Kick, Snapchat, Threads, TikTok, X, YouTube or Twitch, and platforms have purged millions of accounts to comply. This week the High Court began hearing two constitutional challenges to it: one brought by Reddit, the United States online forum, and one by the Sydney-based rights group Digital Freedom Project.

The challengers&rsquo; case rests on the freedom of political communication implied in the Australian Constitution. It is worth being precise about what that freedom is, because it is routinely misdescribed in public commentary. It is not a personal right to speak. It is a restraint on legislative power. The question is therefore not whether a teenager is entitled to an Instagram account, but whether the Commonwealth can justify a law that removes a large cohort from the platforms where a great deal of political conversation now happens.

The Commonwealth is running a harms case. Government lawyer Joanna Davidson told the Court on Wednesday that the ban was necessary to protect developing brains, and set out seven risks said to arise from platform design features: addictive use, adverse sleep effects, depression, anxiety, suicidal ideation, exacerbation of disordered eating and intensification of ADHD symptoms. Reddit&rsquo;s position is that there is no &ldquo;scientific consensus&rdquo; supporting the restriction.

That framing tells you where the case will be won or lost. The purpose is not seriously in dispute. The fight is over evidence and fit: how strong the proof of harm must be, and whether a blunt age-based exclusion is a proportionate way of addressing it.

Status: part heard. No judgment, no orders, no citation. The seven Justices must weigh the harm done by limiting children&rsquo;s political engagement against the harm done by the platforms. New Zealand, the United Kingdom, France and Turkey have all signalled interest in following Australia&rsquo;s lead, so the reasoning will be read well outside this jurisdiction.

Principle to take away: a law that burdens the implied freedom of political communication is valid only if it serves a legitimate purpose by means that are proportionate to that purpose.
