## The rename

"The Day in Court." became **"Recently in Court."** on 10 August 2026, because the window being covered is several days back and the old name implied same day coverage. The new name is accurate whenever the roundup runs, which also means the format no longer depends on a sweep landing the morning after a sitting.

Recorded in `CLAUDE.md`. The old name "The Docket" remains retired and should not be reused.

## Instagram caption (copy-paste)

Five judgments in one sitting day. Here is what came out of the High Court on Wednesday 5 August.

**Mpwerempwer Aboriginal Corporation v Minister** [2026] HCA 23. A licence to take up to 40 gigalitres of groundwater a year for thirty years in the Northern Territory has been set aside. A new condition was imposed requiring an assessment of the impact on Aboriginal cultural values. The company was told and given two days to respond. The native title holders were not told until after the licence had issued. The Court held they should have been heard first.

**Potter (a pseudonym) v The King** [2026] HCA 25. A complainant's covert recording of the accused was lawfully admitted. The Court was unanimous.

Also handed down that day, and worth a look if they touch your subjects:

**The King v HCZ** [2026] HCA 24, youth justice, sentencing of a child for murder and when release before serving 70 per cent of the term is available.

**Plaintiff M98/2025 v Minister** [2026] HCA 26, migration, protection visa, and whether a decision maker has to address a claim that was never expressly made.

**Plaintiff S32/2026 v Minister** [2026] HCA 27, migration.

Two of the five turned on whether the right person was heard at the right time. If you have ever wondered why procedural fairness gets its own fortnight in the syllabus, that is why.
