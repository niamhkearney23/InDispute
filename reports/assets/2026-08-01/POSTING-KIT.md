## LinkedIn post (copy-paste; optionally attach slide 1)

Section 18C just survived its most serious constitutional test in a decade.

On 27 July, the Full Court of the Federal Court unanimously dismissed Senator Pauline Hanson's appeal in the Faruqi litigation. The case began with a September 2022 post telling Senator Mehreen Faruqi to "pack your bags and piss off back to Pakistan", which Justice Stewart found in 2024 to be seriously offensive, a strong form of racism, and a contravention of s 18C of the Racial Discrimination Act (Faruqi v Hanson [2024] FCA 1264).

The appeal was ambitious. Hanson challenged the finding itself, and went further: she argued s 18C is constitutionally invalid as an impermissible burden on the implied freedom of political communication. It is the argument critics of s 18C have made for twenty years, finally run at full strength.

The Full Court rejected all of it. The post was correctly characterised as carrying an anti-Muslim, Islamophobic message, a variant of the historically racist, nativist slogan "go back to where you came from". Section 18C is valid. The fair comment exemption in s 18D was not established.

Three practical observations:

1. The constitutional question is now settled at intermediate appellate level. Only the High Court can disturb it, and Hanson has 28 days to seek special leave.
2. Political context is not a safe harbour. Speech aimed at a person's race or origin does not become protected because the speaker holds office or frames it as politics.
3. Fair comment must be proven, not asserted. Section 18D does real work, but it demands evidence.

If special leave is sought and granted, this becomes the defining speech case of the decade. Either way, advisers should treat s 18C as solid law today.
