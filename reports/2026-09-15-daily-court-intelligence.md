## Lead case

**Coal Mining Industry (Long Service Leave Funding) Corporation v Orica Australia Pty Ltd**, [2026] HCA 30
High Court of Australia, 9 September 2026, Gageler CJ, Gordon, Steward, Gleeson and Jagot JJ
Category: Commercial / Employment / Regulatory

## Confirmed facts

- Coal LSL is the statutory corporation that funds the portable long service leave scheme for Australia's black coal mining industry, via a levy on employers.
- Orica's shotfiring and explosive services employees work at black coal mine sites. Orica argued they fell outside the scheme because of the broader nature of Orica's business.
- The Full Federal Court sided with Orica in 2025 (*Orica Australia Pty Ltd v Coal Mining Industry (Long Service Leave Funding) Corporation* [2025] FCAFC 65), limiting the workers' eligibility.
- Coal LSL was granted special leave and appealed to the High Court, heard 9 April 2026.
- The High Court unanimously allowed Coal LSL's appeal, overturning the Full Federal Court. It held shotfiring is "an intrinsic and essential part of coal mining," and that eligibility under the Act turns on the nature and location of the work performed, not the breadth of the employer's overall business.
- Practical effect: Orica's shotfirers are eligible employees covered by the Black Coal Mining Industry Award, and Orica owes levy contributions accordingly.

**Sources (secondary, cross-checked):** [Law360 Australia](https://www.law360.com/au/articles/2522899), [Lawyerly](https://www.lawyerly.com.au/coal-miners-win-high-court-fight-over-long-service-leave/), [Mining and Energy Union](https://meu.org.au/high-court-victory-secures-long-service-leave-for-shotfirers/), [Mirage News](https://www.miragenews.com/court-win-secures-long-service-leave-for-1741064/).

## Learning line

Learn this: eligibility under an industry-specific statutory scheme turns on the nature and location of the work performed, not the breadth of the employer's overall business.
