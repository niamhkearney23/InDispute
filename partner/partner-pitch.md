## Why this is worth a 30-minute call

Most AML practitioners who go independent spend their first 12 months building what you've already lived through at a Big 4 or Tier 2: a brand, a website, a productised offering, pricing, marketing, lead gen. Then they discover that selling AML services to boutique firms is a different sales problem to the ADI / super / ASX engagements they're used to.

I've done that 12 months of work in the wrong order — built the GTM first, deferred delivery. The result is a working chassis that needs a delivery practitioner inside it.

## Commercial structures (three options)

See `commercial-structures.md` for the detail. In short:

1. **Co-founder / equity.** Substantive equity (negotiable, default starting point 35–40%), vesting over four years, cliff at 12 months. You commit your primary professional time. Lower upfront cash; full upside.

2. **Consulting principal / fee-per-engagement.** No equity. You bill us per delivered engagement (default 45% of Tier 2/3 net fee). You keep your own practice or other commitments. Lower commitment; lower upside; faster to start.

3. **Salaried principal.** Standard senior employment ($180–230k AUD plus bonus). Suits someone leaving a Big 4 for something steadier. Mid-commitment; mid-upside.

I'm open to all three. Honest preference is option 1; option 2 lets us start tomorrow if that's a fit for you.

## The honest version of why I'm doing it this way

I've watched the Tranche 2 wave coming for 18 months. The market opportunity is real and time-bound. I have the GTM instincts and the willingness to build, but I don't have the regulatory depth to credibly deliver Tier 2 and Tier 3 engagements without representing capability I don't have. Pretending otherwise is bad for clients, bad for me, and risks burning the brand before it lands. So I'd rather give up a substantial slice of the business to someone who knows what they're doing than be the kind of consultant who runs ahead of their actual expertise.
