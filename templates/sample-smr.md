## SMR — sample text

**Reporting entity:** {{FIRM_LEGAL_NAME}} (AUSTRAC RE #{{AUSTRAC_RE_ID}})
**Reporting officer:** {{AMLCO_NAME}}, AML/CTF Compliance Officer
**Report date:** [DD MMM YYYY]
**Statutory category:** Suspicious matter (s.41 AML/CTF Act)

### A. The customer
**Customer name:** [Surname], [Given name]
**Date of birth:** [DD/MM/YYYY]
**Address:** [Street], [Suburb], [State] [Postcode]
**Identification used:** [Australian passport / driver licence] · [number]
**Beneficial owner (if entity):** [Name + DOB + address]
**Customer risk rating at time of suspicion:** [Low / Medium / High]
**Date the relationship commenced:** [DD/MM/YYYY]

### B. The designated service
The Firm is acting for [Customer] in the [purchase / sale] of real estate at [property address], settling on [DD/MM/YYYY] for [$X,XXX,XXX].

### C. Grounds for suspicion
The matter triggered the following indicators, set out in chronological order:

1. **On [date]**, the deposit ($XXX,XXX) was received into the Firm's statutory trust account via international wire from [Account name] at [Bank], jurisdiction [Country]. The customer is an Australian resident who, on prior CDD, identified Australia as the source of funds. No connection to [Country] was disclosed at any point in the customer relationship.

2. **On [date]**, the customer requested that the balance ($X,XXX,XXX) be settled partly via [a second third-party entity / cash / cryptocurrency]. When asked the basis for the change, the customer's response was inconsistent with the original engagement letter and was not supported by any documentation.

3. **On [date]**, [Customer] requested that the property be transferred at settlement into the name of [Trust / Company name], a [recently-incorporated / overseas-registered] entity for which the customer is not a named director or beneficiary. No commercial rationale was offered.

4. **On [date]**, beneficial ownership review of the proposed transferee identified ultimate beneficial owner [Name], resident in [Country], who appears on the DFAT Consolidated List as a [designated person].

### D. Reasoning
The combination of (i) an undisclosed cross-border source of funds, (ii) a late and unexplained change in settlement structure, (iii) an opaque transferee entity, and (iv) sanctioned-person exposure on beneficial-owner screening provides reasonable grounds for the Reporting Officer to suspect that the matter relates to or facilitates the laundering of proceeds of crime or the financing of terrorism (s.41(1)(d)–(f)).

### E. Decisions taken
- The Firm has suspended further work on the matter pending the outcome of this report.
- No funds have been transmitted to or from any sanctioned party.
- The Firm has not disclosed the existence or contents of this report to the customer (s.123 tipping-off).
- The Firm has preserved all CDD records, correspondence and decision logs.
- AUSTRAC may direct further action under s.49.

### F. Attachments (referenced internally; not lodged unless requested)
- Engagement letter
- CDD pack (ID, beneficial owner verification, screening results)
- Internal escalation form
- Correspondence log
- Trust account ledger extract
- Sanctions screening result

## Notes for the AMLCO

- Lodge within **3 business days** (24 hours for terrorism-financing).
- Use plain English; avoid legalese where a regulator-readable description will do.
- The "Grounds for suspicion" section is the single most important field — be specific, factual, and chronological.
- If a person within the Firm has formed the suspicion (i.e. not the AMLCO themselves), acknowledge this and identify them.
- Retain a copy of the lodged SMR and the AUSTRAC receipt for 7 years.
